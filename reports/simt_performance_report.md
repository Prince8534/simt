# SIMT Kernel Performance Report

- SIMD/SIMT width: **8 lanes**
- Source: simulation logs generated by `run_simt_regression.sh`
- Efficiency is independently recalculated from lane-cycle counters.
- Ideal packed cycles are a mathematical lower bound, not measured hardware cycles.

| Kernel | Cycles | Useful lane-cycles | Masked lane-cycles | Lane efficiency | Ideal packed-cycle lower bound |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Uniform | 4 | 32 | 0 | 100.00% | 4 |
| Divergent | 6 | 32 | 16 | 66.67% | 4 |
| Nested divergent | 11 | 42 | 46 | 47.73% | 6 |
| Vector addition | 5 | 40 | 0 | 100.00% | 5 |

## Interpretation

- Uniform execution and vector addition report no masked lane-cycles in these tests.
- Divergent execution records masked lane-cycles while the two paths are serialized.
- Nested divergence has additional masked lane-cycles due to nested path execution.
- The ideal packed-cycle lower bound assumes all useful lane work could be perfectly packed into full-width cycles. It does not account for dependencies, control-flow overhead, memory latency, or the actual scheduler.

## Validation

All four logs contained exactly one instance of each required metric and the expected successful-test marker. For every kernel, useful plus masked lane-cycles equaled sampled cycles multiplied by the configured lane count. The printed integer efficiency also matched the independently calculated percentage truncated to an integer.
